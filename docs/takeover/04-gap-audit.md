## "Why not just use Google Classroom? It's free."
Our three strongest answers, based on what the platform **actually does today**:

1. **Exams that survive campus WiFi.** Classroom has no server-side timer and no autosave for a quiz in progress. Here a timed quiz keeps the same server clock on any device. Answers save every few seconds and on reconnect. If a student's phone dies, their saved answers are submitted when time runs out (PR #9; browser test with a simulated WiFi drop, 13/13). That is the difference between a fair internal test and a re-test.
2. **Records the college can defend.** Every student comes from the college roster with a roll number and section (PR #11). Attendance is kept per session. There are weighted gradebooks with rubrics, transcript and analytics exports, an audit log of who changed what, and a one-click DPDP data export for any student. Classroom's records story is "export to Sheets".
3. **The college owns it.** It carries the college's brand, roles that match how the college actually works (dean, course coordinator, faculty, student), invite-only access, and plagiarism checks that run on the server without sending student work to a third party. Classroom requires every student to have a Google account and stores all coursework in Google's storage.

**Where we would be honest with the HoD:** Classroom is better today at the assignment lifecycle (returning work, resubmission, late flags), works with zero setup, and emails people by default. And our "data stays in India" claim is only true once the live database and storage regions are confirmed (rows 2, 3 and 20 in [02-contradictions.md](02-contradictions.md)).
